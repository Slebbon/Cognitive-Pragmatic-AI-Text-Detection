###Future Directions

The repository should be understood as the computational counterpart of the exploratory phase described in the thesis. Its stylometric configuration was intentionally broad and relatively high-dimensional, reflecting a stage in which the objective was to probe feature behaviour, examine prevailing state-of-the-art approaches, and engineer a methodological point of reference—even if not fully stabilised. The aim was to observe how different signals interacted prior to theoretical consolidation. In this sense, as discussed in the thesis, it represents the experimental openness of the research process rather than a finalised and exhaustive benchmark configuration.

Future development may move in the direction of introducing stricter methodological control over this baseline, not to revise its exploratory role, but to stabilise certain aspects of the implementation in light of the refined framework that emerged in later stages of the thesis. The emphasis would be on reinforcing the data-agnostic character of the baseline and reducing unnecessary sources of variability, while preserving interpretability and coherent reproducibility.

One area of potential refinement concerns dependency management and preprocessing stability. As research code evolves, external libraries, linguistic resources, and corpus-dependent normalisations can introduce drift in feature computation. Given the intentionally open configuration of the initial experiment, certain discrepancies between early and later stages have arisen. A new more controlled configuration would therefore aim to minimise such sensitivity by favouring fixed environments and more self-contained feature definitions where feasible independently of attempts to replicate specific state-of-the-art configurations.. The objective is not to constrain flexibility, but to ensure that variability is explicitly documented and methodologically bounded.

A second direction relates to data stratification and evaluation control. During the exploratory phase, data partitioning did undergo iterative adjustments, which in some cases led to issues such as subset inconsistencies or partial overwriting before full consolidation. A future stabilised release would re-apply formalised deterministic procedures to align methodological criteria in the work, ensuring consistent experimental separation and facilitating coherent replication. This formalisation would directly address the reproducibility limitations already acknowledged in the repository documentation and the brittleness mentioned in thesis.

In parallel, the evaluation protocol of the baseline could be progressively aligned with the stricter validation procedures adopted in the later, consolidated experiments of the thesis to reduce statistical variance and improving comparability across experimental stages.

Finally, the feature inventory itself may be streamlined. The high-cardinality stylometric configuration that characterised the exploratory phase may be progressively rationalised toward a consolidated subset requiring more systematic and controlled investigation. This shift would not invalidate the exploratory configuration; rather, it would reposition it as an analytical reference layer, while foregrounding a more interpretable and theoretically coherent core aligned with the thesis conclusions.

Overall, the trajectory of future work is directed toward tightening experimental control and reducing uncontrolled variability, thereby amplifying the stability and data-agnostic properties of the baseline. The repository is intended to remain a transparent research artefact, progressively aligned with the methodological consolidation achieved in the thesis.
